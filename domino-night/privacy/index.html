Domino Night (beta) privacy policy

Last updated October 12, 2026

Domino Night (beta) covers the test versions of our domino party game, on Roku and on Apple TV, iPad and Mac through TestFlight (shown in TestFlight as Mexican Train Night). It's run by John Nelson D'Albis, personally ("we" on this page). It has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking, and we never sell anything about you.

How a game works

The board is on the TV or the host device, and each player uses their phone's browser and types a display name.

On your Wi-Fi (Apple TV, iPad, Mac): the host device runs the game on your local network. Names and the game stay on your Wi-Fi network and are discarded when the host closes the game or quits the app. This connection is not encrypted, so play on a network you trust. Apple versions with online play also stay connected to our server while the host app is open, so the server and Cloudflare records below apply even to their Wi-Fi games.

Online (Roku, and Apple versions with online play): games go through our server, which runs on Cloudflare.

  • What passes through: the names players type, the tiles, moves and scores.
  • How long we keep it: a game's record is deleted within 6 hours. Cloudflare's backups can restore anything we've deleted for up to 30 days. Our server's logs (game codes and events, no names or IP addresses) are kept 7 days.
  • What Cloudflare records: your IP address, device and browser type, and the web addresses used (which include game codes), for at least a month, to run and protect the service.
  • Counts: we keep daily totals of games, split by whether players joined, played online or used a TV (no names, codes or game details), and no other usage statistics.

On devices

A player's phone saves its display name and game codes in its browser so you can rejoin, until you clear the game site's data (play.lazydogsbeach.com, or the address you opened for Wi-Fi games).

On a Roku game, the phone that hosts the game holds it in memory, and the Roku saves the game's codes and access keys (no names) so a game can resume. They're deleted when the game ends normally. If it stops early (for example, the app closes or loses our server), the app resumes it on next opening if it started under about 6 hours ago, and otherwise deletes them. Removing the channel also removes them. On iPad and Mac, the app saves whether sound is muted.

Roku (its privacy policy) and Apple collect information under their own policies. Because we don't track you across sites or apps, Do Not Track signals don't change anything here.

TestFlight

On TestFlight, Apple shares with us your name and email if we invited you by email, your device model, device ID, system version and language, install, session and crash counts, crash logs, and any feedback you send, including screenshots. Screenshots can show player names and game codes. We use this only to run the beta and improve the game, including deciding what goes into the release.

Children

The game asks only for a display name (any nickname works), and never for an age or contact details.

Your information

Game records on our server delete themselves (see above), and you can clear your phone's saved name yourself. To ask about anything else, email help@lazydogsbeach.com.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above.
